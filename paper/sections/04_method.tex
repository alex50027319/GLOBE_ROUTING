\section{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}
Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the proposed workflow. A privileged teacher is trained offline with global graph information. A student receives local observations and learns to imitate teacher action distributions. The deployed policy combines a nominal distilled branch with a predictive branch through a risk switch. The Phase 13 \method\ extension adds link-loss-aware risk, top-$k$ onward stability, energy-aware tie breaking, and drop suppression.

\begin{figure*}[t]

\caption{System architecture of the proposed GLOBE++ Lite-P+ routing pipeline. The global teacher is used only for offline privileged training and distillation, while the deployed router selects the next hop from local one-hop observations through a nominal branch, a predictive branch, and a risk-switch decision.}

\subsection{Global Teacher}
The teacher policy $\pi_T(a\mid s_t)$ observes global topology state $s_t$, including graph structure, node positions, mobility, queue features, and destination information. In the current implementation, the teacher is implemented as a global actor-critic model and trained with PPO during the earlier phases of the project. The teacher is not deployed. Its role is to generate routing behavior and soft targets:
\begin{equation}
    y_T(a)=\mathrm{softmax}\left(\frac{z_T(a)}{T}\right),
\end{equation}
where $z_T(a)$ is the teacher logit for action $a$ and $T$ is the distillation temperature.

\subsection{Reinforcement-Learning-Based Global Teacher Training}
\label{subsec:teacher_rl}
The reinforcement-learning component of the proposed framework is used to train the privileged global teacher before student distillation. At training step $t$, the teacher observes the global state
\begin{equation}
s_t=(\mathcal{G}_t,p_t,v_t,q_t,d),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{G}_t$ is the FANET topology, $p_t$ and $v_t$ are node positions and velocities, $q_t$ is the queue state, and $d$ is the packet destination. The teacher samples or selects a next-hop action according to
\begin{equation}
a_t \sim \pi_T(a_t\mid s_t;\theta_T),
\end{equation}
and receives a routing reward from the simulator. A typical reward combines delivery success, delay, energy, hop count, and failure penalties:
\begin{equation}
r_t
=
\alpha_{\mathrm{succ}}R_{\mathrm{succ}}
-\alpha_DD_t
-\alpha_EE_t
-\alpha_HH_t
-\alpha_FF_t .
\end{equation}
Here $R_{\mathrm{succ}}$ rewards successful packet delivery, $D_t$ penalizes delay, $E_t$ is an energy or transmission-cost proxy, $H_t$ penalizes excessive hops, and $F_t$ penalizes route failure, drop, or loop events.

The teacher objective is to maximize the expected discounted return:
\begin{equation}
J(\theta_T)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_T}
\left[
\sum_{t=0}^{T_{\mathrm{ep}}}\gamma^t r_t
\right],
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ is the discount factor and $T_{\mathrm{ep}}$ is the episode horizon. In the current implementation, this objective is optimized with a PPO-style actor-critic update. The policy-ratio term is
\begin{equation}
\rho_t(\theta_T)
=
\frac{
\pi_T(a_t\mid s_t;\theta_T)
}{
\pi_T(a_t\mid s_t;\theta_T^{\mathrm{old}})
},
\end{equation}
and the advantage estimate is
\begin{equation}
A_t
=
\hat{R}_t
-
V_T(s_t;\phi_T),
\end{equation}
where $\hat{R}_t$ is the empirical or bootstrapped return and $V_T$ is the teacher value function. The clipped PPO policy loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PPO}}
=
-
\mathbb{E}_t
\left[
\min
\left(
\rho_t(\theta_T)A_t,\;
\mathrm{clip}(\rho_t(\theta_T),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_t
\right)
\right].
\end{equation}
The clipping parameter $\epsilon$ prevents the teacher policy from changing too aggressively in a single update. The value function is trained by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{V}
=
\mathbb{E}_t
\left[
\left(
V_T(s_t;\phi_T)-\hat{R}_t
\right)^2
\right],
\end{equation}
and the total teacher-training loss can be written as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{T}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PPO}}
+
c_V\mathcal{L}_{V}
-
c_H\mathcal{H}(\pi_T),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{H}(\pi_T)$ is an entropy bonus that encourages exploration during teacher training.

After convergence, the trained teacher parameters $\theta_T^\star$ are frozen. The teacher is then used only to generate soft routing targets for states encountered in rollout data:
\begin{equation}
\pi_T(\cdot\mid s_t;\theta_T^\star)
\rightarrow
\text{distillation target for }
\pi_S(\cdot\mid o_u;\theta_S).
\end{equation}
Thus, reinforcement learning is not performed on deployed UAVs. It is an offline mechanism for learning globally informed routing behavior, which is subsequently compressed into a local student policy.

\subsection{Local Student Distillation}
The student policy $\pi_S(a\mid o_u)$ observes only local candidate features. The distillation objective combines soft teacher imitation, hard action imitation, and optional oracle/path targets:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}}
=
\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}T^2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(y_T\Vert y_S)
+\lambda_{\mathrm{CE}}\mathrm{CE}(a_T,\pi_S)
+\lambda_{\mathrm{O}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}},
\end{equation}
where $y_S=\mathrm{softmax}(z_S/T)$, $a_T=\arg\max_a \pi_T(a\mid s_t)$, and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ biases the student toward feasible shortest-path or recovery actions when such labels are available.

\subsection{Predictive Candidate Features}
For each candidate next hop $i$, the risk branch uses
\begin{equation}
x_i^{\mathrm{risk}}=[m_i,\ell_i,q_i,o_i],
\end{equation}
where $m_i$ is link margin, $\ell_i$ is predicted current-link lifetime, $q_i$ is queue headroom, and $o_i$ is best onward-link lifetime after selecting candidate $i$.

The Phase 13 P+ extension adds
\begin{equation}
x_i^{+}=[\bar{o}_{i,\mathrm{top}k},\rho_i,p_{i,\mathrm{keep}},\eta_i],
\end{equation}
where $\bar{o}_{i,\mathrm{top}k}$ is the average of the top-$k$ onward link lifetimes, $\rho_i$ is normalized onward redundancy, $p_{i,\mathrm{keep}}$ is link survival probability under stochastic link loss, and
\begin{equation}
\eta_i=1-\left(\frac{d_i}{R_c}\right)^2
\end{equation}
is an energy-efficiency proxy for distance $d_i$ between the current UAV and candidate $i$.

\subsection{Risk Score and Safety Gain}
The P+ danger score is
\begin{align}
D_i &=
[g_m-m_i]_+
+[g_\ell-\ell_i]_+
+[g_o-o_i]_+ \nonumber\\
&\quad +[g_k-\bar{o}_{i,\mathrm{top}k}]_+
+0.5[g_\rho-\rho_i]_+
+[g_p-p_{i,\mathrm{keep}}]_+,
\end{align}
where $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$ and the $g$ terms are safety gates.

The candidate safety utility is
\begin{equation}
Q_i=m_i+\ell_i+o_i+\bar{o}_{i,\mathrm{top}k}+0.5\rho_i+p_{i,\mathrm{keep}}.
\end{equation}
For a predictive action $a_P$ and nominal action $a_N$, the safety gain is
\begin{equation}
G(a_P,a_N)=Q_{a_P}-Q_{a_N}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Risk-Switch Decision}
The nominal branch is used by default. The predictive branch is activated only when the nominal action is unsafe:
\begin{equation}
S=\mathbb{1}\left[
a_N=\dropact
\vee
D_{a_N}>\tau
\vee
\left(a_P\neq a_N \land G(a_P,a_N)>\delta \land D_{a_P}<D_{a_N}\right)
\right].
\end{equation}
The final action is
\begin{equation}
a^\star=
\begin{cases}
a_P, & S=1,\\
a_N, & S=0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Energy Tie Breaking and Drop Suppression}
The predictive logit is adjusted by an energy tie term:
\begin{equation}
z_i^{P+}=z_i^P+\lambda_E\eta_i.
\end{equation}
This term is intended to choose shorter and more energy-efficient links when safety is similar.

If at least one safe forwarding candidate exists, the drop logit is reduced:
\begin{equation}
z_{\dropact}^{P+}=z_{\dropact}-\lambda_D.
\end{equation}
This does not force forwarding in all cases; it only discourages premature dropping when a locally safe candidate is available.
