\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Flying Ad Hoc Networks (FANETs) have emerged as a pivotal technology for enabling rapidly deployable communication infrastructures, collaborative sensing swarms, and resilient emergency networks in environments lacking fixed ground facilities. In these networks, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) serve as highly mobile routing nodes that dynamically establish wireless links to forward packets in a multi-hop fashion. However, achieving reliable packet routing in FANETs remains an open challenge. The extremely dynamic nature of UAV mobility causes rapid topology transitions, resulting in frequent link disruptions and packet drops. Furthermore, each UAV operates under severe hardware and energy constraints, preventing them from maintaining a global view of the entire network or executing heavy coordination protocols. Consequently, a practical FANET routing policy must resolve a fundamental conflict: it must exploit rich topology and mobility information to avoid transient and unstable routes, while remaining lightweight enough to be executed in a completely decentralized manner.

Traditional routing protocols in mobile ad hoc networks (MANETs) and vehicular ad hoc networks (VANETs), such as AODV and OLSR, struggle to adapt to the three-dimensional mobility and high velocity of UAVs, often incurring prohibitive signaling overhead during route discovery. As an alternative, geographic routing protocols, exemplified by Greedy Perimeter Stateless Routing (GPSR)~\cite{gpsr}, select the next hop solely based on the physical coordinates of neighboring nodes. Although geographic routing is highly scalable and low-overhead, it frequently suffers from the "routing hole" problem, where packets become trapped in local dead ends with no neighbor closer to the destination than the forwarding node itself. Moreover, greedy next-hop decisions ignore critical factors such as wireless channel stability, queue congestion, and link lifetimes, leading to premature link breaks when forwarding through geographically optimal but highly unstable links.

To overcome these structural limitations, recent researchers have proposed reinforcement learning (RL) and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) frameworks for FANET routing. By formulating the routing problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), these approaches learn to balance multiple network metrics, including delivery success, queue buffers, and link stability. However, deploying these learned models on resource-constrained UAV swarms reveals a severe "deployment gap". High-performance MARL models often rely on centralized training with centralized execution, or require continuous online message exchange (e.g., emergent communication in DRAMA~\cite{drama}) to maintain cooperative behavior. Such dependencies introduce massive communication overhead, making them impractical for sparse, high-speed, and interference-prone UAV deployments. 

In this paper, we bridge this deployment gap by presenting \method, a global-to-local routing framework that achieves high reliability with strictly decentralized, communication-free execution. Our framework leverages the Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm through policy distillation. During offline training, a privileged global teacher policy is trained via reinforcement learning (PPO) using full topology and mobility information. This teacher policy is then compressed into a local student policy using knowledge distillation, training the student to predict optimal routing utilities using only one-hop neighbor features. During execution, the deployed policy uses a hybrid decision rule called "Predictive Risk Switching". By default, the UAV executes a nominal distilled geographic policy to minimize latency and control overhead. However, when local safety features indicate elevated risks—such as routing holes, buffer overflow, or imminent link breaks—a predictive branch is selectively activated to bypass the critical node.

Building upon the previous \phaseTwelve\ baseline, the proposed \method\ framework introduces four key enhancements specifically designed to handle stochastic link failures: link-loss-aware risk switching, top-$k$ onward stability estimation, energy-aware tie-breaking, and drop suppression. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate decentralized FANET routing as a Dec-POMDP next-hop decision problem, separating training-time privileged global information from execution-time local observations to ensure complete deployability.
    \item We design a robust global-to-local policy distillation pipeline where a global teacher actor-critic network transfers its routing heuristics to a lightweight local student via action distribution alignment using Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence.
    \item We propose a predictive risk switching mechanism, a selective safety gate that activates a predictive policy branch only when local features (link margin, link lifetime, onward stability, redundancy, and link survival probability) indicate high risk, thereby preserving low-overhead execution in benign situations.
    \item We present a comprehensive evaluation across 14 distinct scenarios and five random seeds, demonstrating that the proposed framework outperforms GPSR, Predictive Geographic Routing, Evo-QGeo, IQMR, and DRAMA in terms of Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR) and deadline delivery, while maintaining a low control-byte overhead.
    \item We explicitly map out the remaining evidence gaps for a submission-quality manuscript, outlining the planned Phase 13 P+ validation, energy-model calibrations, and statistical significance tests.
\end{itemize}
