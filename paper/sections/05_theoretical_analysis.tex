\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

In this section, we analyze the theoretical limits of global-to-local policy distillation and formalize how the proposed predictive risk switching mechanism mitigates execution errors under partial observability.

\subsection{Local Deployability and Information Constraints}
A decentralized routing policy is locally deployable if it can compute forwarding decisions using only parameters accessible within a local one-hop neighborhood. By design, the student policy inputs $o_u(t)$ and the predictive risk metrics ($m_i, \ell_i, q_i, o_i, \bar{o}_{i,\mathrm{top}k}, \rho_i, p_{i,\mathrm{keep}}, \eta_i$) require no multi-hop signaling or centralized coordination. Thus, the computational complexity of the deployed student policy remains $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{N}_u(t)|)$ in terms of the number of forwarding candidates, allowing real-time execution on resource-constrained UAV flight controllers.

\subsection{Decomposition of Policy Error}
Let $\pi_T(a \mid s)$ be the optimal privileged teacher policy and $\pi_S(a \mid o)$ be the distilled local student policy. The total routing performance mismatch, represented as the policy error $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{total}}$, arises from two distinct sources:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{total}} = \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{info}} + \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{approx}},
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Information-Gap Error} ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{info}}$): The error introduced because the student state mapping $o = \mathcal{O}(s, u)$ is a non-injective function of the global network state $s$. Multiple global topologies map to identical local observations (observation aliasing), creating an irreducible loss of policy performance:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{info}} = \mathbb{E}_{s \sim d^{\pi_S}} \left[ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_T(\cdot \mid s) \Vert \pi_T(\cdot \mid \mathcal{O}(s))) \right],
    \end{equation}
    where $\pi_T(\cdot \mid \mathcal{O}(s))$ is the projection of the teacher policy onto the local observation space.
    \item \textbf{Approximation and Optimization Error} ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{approx}}$): The error stemming from the finite capacity of the student model (e.g., MLP depth and width) and local minima during training:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{approx}} = \mathbb{E}_{o \sim d^{\pi_S}} \left[ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_T(\cdot \mid o) \Vert \pi_S(\cdot \mid o)) \right].
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Performance Bound and the Role of Risk Switching}
Under the Performance Difference Lemma, the return gap $\Delta J = J(\pi_T) - J(\pi_S)$ can be bounded by the expected policy divergence over the state occupancy distribution induced by the student policy $d^{\pi_S}$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta J \le \frac{1}{1-\gamma} \mathbb{E}_{s \sim d^{\pi_S}} \left[ \sqrt{2 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_T(\cdot \mid s) \Vert \pi_S(\cdot \mid \mathcal{O}(s)))} \right].
\end{equation}
Under standard knowledge distillation, minimizing average KL-divergence does not guarantee a small return gap $\Delta J$. In dynamic FANETs, routing failures (such as routing holes or link breaks) represent rare, out-of-distribution states. The student policy, optimized for average-case distillation loss, often executes poor actions in these critical states, leading to catastrophic path failures.

The proposed Predictive Risk Switching (PRS) mechanism selectively relaxes this bound. Let $S \in \{0, 1\}$ be the switch variable. In benign routing states where the nominal local danger score $D_{a_N} \le \tau$, we enforce $S=0$, yielding:
\begin{equation}
    \Pr(S=1 \mid D_{a_N} \le \tau) \approx 0.
\end{equation}
In this regime, the system operates under the nominal distilled policy, minimizing execution latency. When the nominal candidate enters a high-danger state ($D_{a_N} > \tau$), the PRS activates ($S=1$), bypassing the nominal policy and choosing the predictive candidate $a_P$ that maximizes safety gain. Consequently, the PRS restricts the occupancy of high-danger states in $d^{\pi_S}$, bounding the worst-case divergence $\Delta J$ during topology disruptions.
