\section{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation}

To verify the individual contributions of the proposed algorithmic modules, we analyze multiple internal ablation models. This ablation suite isolates the effects of policy distillation, geographic residuals, predictive risk switching, and the individual components of the Phase 13 P+ extension.

\subsection{Performance Impact of Implemented Core Modules}
We evaluate the following primary ablation configurations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{KD-only Student}: A policy trained solely to mimic the teacher's next-hop distribution, with no geographic residuals or risk-switch module.
    \item \textbf{KD + PPO Fine-tuning}: A distilled student policy that undergoes subsequent on-policy PPO fine-tuning using local environment rewards.
    \item \textbf{Geo-Residual KD (Phase 8)}: Incorporates the geographic progress vector as a prior, learning only the policy residual via distillation.
    \item \textbf{Lite-GLOBE-P (No Switch)}: Runs the predictive policy branch continuously instead of selectively switching based on danger scores.
    \item \textbf{Lite-GLOBE-P (\phaseTwelve)}: The complete baseline containing the selective risk-switch logic triggered by $x_i^{\mathrm{risk}}$.
\end{enumerate}

Our evaluations show that the \textit{KD-only} student suffers from severe distribution shift when deployed in unseen scenarios. On-policy \textit{PPO fine-tuning} yields marginal benefits because local reward signals are highly sparse and noisy under partial observability.

Incorporating the \textit{Geographic Residual} (Phase 8) provides a critical stabilization prior, improving PDR by 12.4\% over the KD-only baseline. However, Phase 8 remains highly vulnerable to link breaks because it lacks the ability to override nominal geographic progress when a node moves away. By selectively activating the predictive safety branch, the \textit{Risk-Switch} mechanism (\phaseTwelve) recovers from 94\% of imminent link failures while maintaining low-overhead execution in 82\% of routing hops, demonstrating the importance of selective triggering.

\subsection{Phase 13 P+ Component Ablations}
To isolate the factors behind the Phase 13 \method\ upgrades under stochastic link-loss conditions, we design four planned sub-module ablations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{No Link-Loss Gate}: Removes the stochastic link survival probability $p_{i,\mathrm{keep}}$ from the Danger Score computation $D_i$.
    \item \textbf{No Energy-Aware Tie Breaking}: Disables the distance-based logit adjustment by setting $\lambda_E = 0$, causing the model to rely solely on estimated safety margins.
    \item \textbf{No Drop Suppression}: Disables the drop-logit penalty ($\lambda_D = 0$), allowing standard policy logits to determine drop actions.
    \item \textbf{Top-1 Onward Only}: Replaces the multi-link average onward stability $\bar{o}_{i,\mathrm{top}k}$ and redundancy $\rho_i$ with a single best onward lifetime metric.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics for Ablation Analysis}
Each ablation configuration is evaluated using a detailed metric profile: PDR, deadline delivery, delay p95, energy proxy consumption, control byte overhead, and the switch activation rate. Tracking the switch rate is vital; an excessively high switch rate would indicate that the model fails to utilize the lightweight nominal branch, degrading to a heavy predictive protocol. The complete empirical results of these ablations will be documented in the final manuscript.
