\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{FANETs and Classical Geographic Routing}
Routing in Flying Ad Hoc Networks (FANETs) is exceptionally challenging due to the highly dynamic three-dimensional movement of UAV nodes, rapid changes in link quality, and frequent network partitioning. Traditional topology-based routing protocols (e.g., proactive OLSR and reactive AODV) suffer from heavy control overhead and slow convergence under fast topology changes. To alleviate these limitations, geographic routing protocols, such as Greedy Perimeter Stateless Routing (GPSR)~\cite{gpsr}, have been widely adopted. Geographic routing makes localized forwarding decisions based on the positions of the current node, its one-hop neighbors, and the destination node, eliminating the need for global routing tables. However, pure greedy forwarding struggles in the presence of "routing voids" or holes, where packets become trapped at local minima. While perimeter mode (face routing) can bypass holes, it often results in excessively long paths, high latency, and high packet drop rates in three-dimensional mobile topologies.

\subsection{Predictive Geographic Routing}
To improve the robustness of geographic routing, predictive geographic protocols integrate mobility prediction and link-state estimation. By exploiting node velocities and trajectories, these protocols estimate metrics such as Link Expiration Time (LET) or Link Stability. Recent advancements, such as Evo-QGeo~\cite{evoqgeo}, model the future evolution of link states to proactively route packets around prospective holes and unstable links. While predictive routing significantly improves Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR) in mobile scenarios, it typically relies on deterministic mobility models or periodic exchange of large control packets to maintain accurate predictions. In contrast, our proposed \method\ framework integrates predictive features (e.g., link survival probability, onward link stability) into a localized reinforcement learning policy, avoiding heavy control message overhead and adapting to stochastic link perturbations.

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning and MARL in FANET Routing}
Reinforcement learning (RL) and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) have emerged as powerful frameworks for learning adaptive routing policies under uncertainty. In these schemes, next-hop selection is formulated as a decision-making process where agents receive rewards based on successful packet delivery, latency, queue buffering, and energy consumption. For instance, Q-routing algorithms learn optimal paths dynamically by updating local Q-tables based on temporal difference learning. In multi-agent settings, algorithms like DRAMA~\cite{drama} utilize emergent communication to establish cooperative routing strategies among neighboring nodes. However, deploying cooperative MARL policies in actual UAV swarms is severely constrained by the overhead of online message passing and the susceptibility of decentralized policies to training instability.

\subsection{Graph Neural Networks and CTDE}
Since network topologies naturally form graphs, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have been successfully applied to extract spatial and structural network features for routing. Under the Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm, policies can be trained using global graph topology information and subsequently executed on individual nodes using only local neighborhood observations. Studies like GLo-MAPPO~\cite{glo_mappo} demonstrate the efficacy of CTDE in optimizing energy-efficient UAV networks. However, directly executing GNNs or complex cooperative policies online on resource-constrained UAV hardware remains computationally expensive. Our work addresses this by utilizing policy distillation, transferring the knowledge of a global graph-based GNN teacher into a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) student policy that executes in constant time.

\subsection{Policy Distillation in Networking}
Knowledge Distillation (KD) and Policy Distillation (PD) compress high-capacity teacher models into compact student models by minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between their output probability distributions. In reinforcement learning, policy distillation has been widely used to compress policies or transfer multi-task capabilities. In the context of ad hoc routing, distilling the action distribution (soft next-hop selection probabilities) carries crucial relative preferences among multiple candidate neighbors. This soft probability distribution captures the teacher's global structural knowledge, guiding the student to select paths that are not only geographically close but also stable and less prone to congestion, even when the student only has access to one-hop features.
